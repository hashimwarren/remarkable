# Rhetorical Contract

## Purpose

Preserve the approved persuasive choices that give an article its distinctive movement. The rhetorical contract lives inside the canonical outline; it is editorial memory, not a separate artifact, visible taxonomy, or rigid prose template.

## Record the contract in the outline

For each consequential section, place one italic editorial sentence directly beneath its heading. That sentence must name, in natural language:

- the rhetorical move the section will make;
- the intended effect on the reader; and
- why that move advances this governing premise.

Use the contract only where a choice materially shapes the reading experience: headline and opening, central turn or reveal, proof, framework when present, and closing. Do not annotate every paragraph or expose copywriting labels merely to demonstrate technique.

Example:

```markdown
## Opening

*Open with the founder getting different results from the same model, creating the question of what an experienced writer is supplying that the founder is not.*

- Establish the apparently contradictory outcomes.
- Ground the contrast in a specific scene.
- Preserve the explanation for the planned turn.

## Proof

*Move from personal observation to independent evidence so the reader can accept the mechanism without relying on the writer's authority alone.*

## Framework

*Resolve the opening question with a practical system that makes the invisible editorial choices repeatable.*

## Closing

*Return to the two writers using the same model and show what the founder can now do differently.*
```

The bullets describe content and sequence. The italic sentence governs how and why the section performs its job. Headings should enact the chosen movement rather than merely label a topic.

## Authority order

When instructions, edits, or diagnostics conflict, use this order:

1. Governing premise and argument
2. Factual meaning and calibrated certainty
3. Approved rhetorical contract and writer voice
4. Local prose-hygiene findings

No sentence-level improvement may silently weaken a higher authority.

## Preserve or renegotiate

Drafting and editing may strengthen execution without another checkpoint when the selected move remains intact. A change of rhetorical direction requires the writer's approval.

Before proposing a different direction:

1. Diagnose why the approved direction is not working.
2. Name the alternative in plain language.
3. Explain what it gains and what it sacrifices.
4. Offer **Strengthen this direction**, **Explore a different direction**, or **Keep it as written**.
5. If the writer chooses a different direction, update the outline's italic contract sentence first, reset `Status: working`, revise the relevant outline bullets, and obtain outline approval before materially rewriting the article.

Never rewrite the outline after the fact merely to legitimize drift that already occurred in the draft.

## Shared focused-command behavior

Headline, opening, proof, framework, and closing work must:

1. Read `PREMISE.md`, the approved outline, and the current article when available.
2. Diagnose the section's current rhetorical job and whether it succeeds.
3. Preserve a strong existing section instead of manufacturing alternatives.
4. When change is warranted, offer materially different directions and explain briefly how each would advance the premise.
5. Let the writer choose, combine, revise, or keep the original.
6. Validate the accepted change against the premise, evidence boundary, and the rest of the contract.
7. Update the canonical outline before the article whenever the accepted change alters rhetorical direction.

Focused commands patch only the relevant canonical section. They do not create alternate articles or standalone contract artifacts.

## Cross-stage integrity check

After drafting, Slopless revisions, accepted critique edits, or focused section work, verify:

- the selected move is still recognizable;
- its intended effect is at least as strong;
- the premise, meaning, certainty, and emphasis have not shifted accidentally;
- the section still connects to the surrounding argument; and
- the prose has not reverted toward a generic professional register.

The contract protects intentional choices; it does not protect weak execution. Strengthen a selected direction when possible, and renegotiate it openly when necessary.
