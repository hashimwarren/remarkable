# Informational Images

Use LENS privately to decide whether an article needs an image and what intellectual job it should perform. Images and headlines both earn attention and frame meaning; an informational image must also help the reader inspect, understand, compare, or use the argument.

## Choose the image job

- **Legitimacy:** make evidence inspectable through a source excerpt, screenshot, documented artifact, demonstration, or before-and-after.
- **Explanation:** reveal a mechanism, relationship, process, distinction, or framework that prose makes harder to hold in mind.
- **Numbers:** make a verified comparison, distribution, trend, or magnitude visible through a chart, table, or annotated measure.
- **Steps:** make application easier through a checklist, sequence, decision tree, annotated example, or reference card.

Ask privately: Does the reader need to believe it, compare it, understand it, or use it? If prose already does the job better, choose no image.

## Offer images at two earned moments

- After an approved framework, privately test **Explanation** and **Steps**. Offer image work only when the framework has a concrete visual job.
- During proof, after claim and section jobs are stable, privately test **Legitimacy** and **Numbers**. Offer image work only when verified evidence would become more inspectable or comprehensible.

When a job is earned, make a one-sentence offer that names the proposed form and what it would let the reader understand or inspect. Use these structured choices when available, with the same plain-text fallback:

- **Make this image** — authorizes the brief and production for this named image.
- **Plan it for later** — authorizes only the brief and placeholder, not production.
- **Keep it as prose** — records no visual and continues without weakening the article.

This is an optional-but-real checkpoint: it appears only when an image job is earned, and the agent must STOP and wait after showing it. If no concrete job survives, say nothing and continue. Do not expose the LENS acronym by default and do not fill an image quota.

## Route production honestly

- Legitimacy uses real, attributable artifacts rather than generated documentary evidence.
- Numbers uses deterministic charts, tables, or calculations from verified data; never generate chart values as artwork.
- Explanation may use a diagram or annotated conceptual image.
- Steps may use a checklist, decision tree, sequence, annotated example, or reference card.

Only **Make this image** or another explicit production request authorizes generation. When the writer chooses **Plan it for later**, production was not requested, or the needed tool is unavailable, preserve a low-fidelity textual placeholder and production specification. Read [visual-placeholders.md](visual-placeholders.md) for asset handling.

## Write a compact visual brief

Record only accepted visual decisions in the working outline:

```markdown
- **Visual job:** [Legitimacy, Explanation, Numbers, or Steps]
- **Claim or section:** [What it advances]
- **Reader should notice:** [Single intended realization]
- **Source material:** [Verified sources, data, or none]
- **Format:** [Chart, screenshot, diagram, decision tree, etc.]
- **Truth constraints:** [What must not be implied or invented]
- **Placement and caption job:** [Where it appears and what the caption adds]
- **Why visual:** [Why prose alone is weaker]
```

## Score privately before preserving

Score each concept 0–2 on Necessity, Added information, Inspection, Focus, Proximity, Fidelity, and Compression. A concept below about 9/14 should be redesigned or removed. A zero for Fidelity blocks publishable use regardless of total score. Keep the score private; store only the brief and decision.

The header-image concept is editorial framing, not automatically LENS evidence. It may express the central tension or promise, but it must not impersonate proof.
